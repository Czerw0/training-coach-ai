# Gym — strength-endurance (loaded vertical)

> DRAFT — coach to verify. General strength-and-conditioning guidance, not
> individualised medical advice.

## Use when

Sustained leg work under load: **stair machine, weighted step-ups, uphill
treadmill hikes, loaded carries, high-rep circuits.** It sits between the gym and
the endurance library — muscular endurance and local fatigue resistance in the
legs, with a real cardiovascular cost, at moderate impact. Reach for it to:

- Combine endurance and strength in one session when the week is tight.
- Train **vertical-gain capacity** and quad/glute endurance directly.
- Substitute for a lower-body gym day when a lower-impact, more aerobic leg
  stimulus is wanted than heavy lifting (the athlete can swap a lower-gym slot
  for this).

> **This athlete —** this is a **ski-specific** session: the stair machine and
> loaded uphill work train exactly the sustained quad/glute endurance and
> vertical capacity that alpine and ski-touring days demand, and weighted
> step-downs add the eccentric/braking element of descents. It also doubles as
> hiking/mountaineering-specific preparation when a mountain trip is coming up.
> Because it is weight-bearing endurance rather than pure strength, it counts
> toward the week's leg-impact and metabolic budget — don't treat it as free.

## Don't use when

- A knee or Achilles is reactive — loaded stepping and step-downs concentrate
  load exactly there. Use `gym/lower-isometric` or `cycling/endurance` instead.
- Systemic illness symptoms are present.
- The same day as hard cycling/running or heavy/eccentric leg gym — the shared
  metabolic and leg cost means both sessions suffer.
- Maximal force is the block's goal — this builds endurance of force, not peak
  force; pair it with, don't replace, `gym/lower-max-strength` when strength is
  the priority.

## Prescription

| Variant | Structure | Load / intensity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stair machine | 20–40 min | steady, Zone 2–3 HR, optional vest | The default; sustained, conversational-to-working |
| Stair intervals | 4–6 × 3–4 min | harder efforts, easy between | When a harder metabolic dose is wanted |
| Weighted step-up | 3–4 × 10–15/leg | vest or DBs, controlled | Add slow step-**downs** for eccentric/braking |
| Uphill treadmill hike | 30–60 min | steep grade, pack optional | Closest to a mountain day; trip-specific prep |
| Loaded carry circuit | 4–6 × 30–60 s | heavy carries + step-ups | Trunk + grip + leg endurance |

**Discipline:** the steady variants stay steady. If the stair machine drifts into
a gasping effort it becomes a `cycling/intervals`-grade metabolic session and
must be spaced like one — decide up front which it is.

## Dosing and progression

- **Add duration, then load (vest/pack), then grade/intensity** — one variable
  at a time. Frequency and time-under-load before you add weight.
- Progress the long/hard version against its own recent ceiling, not a weekly
  percentage (`principles/load-and-recovery` §3).
- Step-**downs** (eccentric) ramp conservatively — they are the sorest element;
  add a little range/load per week, not a lot.
- **Regression path:** shorter/unloaded → steady only → `cycling/endurance`.

## Scheduling

**Hard rules:**
- Not the same day as anything in `same_day_conflicts` (hard aerobic, heavy or
  eccentric legs, power).
- Keep ≥24 h from hard rides/runs and from power work — it pre-fatigues the legs
  and competes metabolically.

**Preferences:**
- Mid-week, or as the "second endurance-ish day" in a week built around cycling.
- Pairs cleanly the day before/after `gym/upper-body` or `gym/lower-isometric`.
- Excellent as trip-specific prep in the weeks before a hike/mountain day; taper
  it in the last ~48 h before the trip itself.
- The day after is fine for easy Z2 riding; keep hard leg work off it.

**What it costs the next 24–36 h:** high `metabolic` (clears within a day),
moderate `mechanical` and `damage` from the stepping/carrying. Legs feel worked
rather than pounded — but the step-down/eccentric element can leave real DOMS if
progressed too fast.

## Interactions

- **With `cycling/endurance`:** complementary — different enough that a Z2 ride
  the day after is a genuine flush, not more of the same.
- **With `cycling/intervals` and hard running:** shared metabolic cost; keep them
  apart or the week has too many hard aerobic days
  (`principles/planning-the-week` §2).
- **With `gym/lower-max-strength`:** pair, don't replace, when strength is the
  goal — this trains endurance of force, that trains peak force.
- **With hiking/mountaineering days:** this is the closest gym proxy; use it to
  build the capacity a big mountain day will demand.
- **Interference:** a mixed endurance/strength stimulus at moderate load; no
  strong interference concern, but it does add to the leg-fatigue budget the
  power and plyo sessions need protected.

## Stop rules

- Knee or Achilles pain during loaded stepping — stop; this session concentrates
  load there and is the wrong tool for a reactive joint.
- Rising `damage`/soreness from previous step-down work still present — postpone
  the eccentric element rather than stacking it.
- Disproportionate breathlessness, chest symptoms or dizziness — stop.
- Form collapsing on step-ups/downs (knee caving, dropping rather than lowering)
  — the set is over.

## Worked example

> **Context:** this athlete, late-August, cycling-primary week (two rides
> planned) but wants a second leg stimulus that supports rather than fights the
> riding, and a mountain hike is three weeks out. Readiness normal.

> **Prescription — Thursday:**
> - Stair machine, 30 min steady at Zone 2–3, weighted vest (~8 kg)
> - Weighted step-ups 3 × 12/leg, with a controlled 3 s step-**down**
> - Suitcase carry 4 × 40 s
> - Kept steady, not gasping — this is the endurance/strength blend, not a
>   metabolic interval day.

> **Placement rationale:** slots as the week's second leg day without adding a
> hard aerobic session — it sits ≥24 h from both rides and clear of any power/
> eccentric work. The vest and step-downs build the vertical-gain and braking
> capacity the coming mountain day needs. In this cycling-primary week it stays
> submaximal so the riding remains the priority.

## Evidence notes

**Supported:**
- Muscular-endurance and strength-endurance qualities are trainable distinctly
  from maximal strength, and sustained loaded work develops local fatigue
  resistance — relevant to prolonged weight-bearing sports (hiking, ski touring,
  alpine descents).
- Weight-bearing endurance carries a higher mechanical/impact cost per aerobic
  minute than cycling — the reason it counts toward the leg-tissue budget rather
  than being "free" aerobic volume (cf. `running/easy-long` Evidence notes).

**Convention (defaults, thinly evidenced):**
- The specific durations, rep ranges and the 24 h spacing floors. Sensible
  coaching defaults matched to the intensity, not tested thresholds.
- Treating the steady stair machine as Zone 2–3 — anchor to the athlete's own HR
  response; loaded stepping drives HR higher than the muscular effort suggests.
