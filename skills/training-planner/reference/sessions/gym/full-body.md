# Gym — full body, all-round

> DRAFT — coach to verify. General strength-and-conditioning guidance, not
> individualised medical advice.

## Use when

One balanced session that trains the whole body — a lower push/pull (squat or
hinge), an upper push and an upper pull, and trunk/anti-rotation work — at
moderate load. It exists for the weeks that only have room for **one** gym slot
but still need both legs and upper trained, and as a rotation partner to the
more specialised lower-body days (`gym/lower-max-strength`, `gym/lower-eccentric`,
`gym/lower-isometric`, `gym/lower-power`) so the athlete gets varied stimulus
across a block rather than the same squat session every week.

> **This athlete —** skiing is the winter goal, so the leg half of this session
> should always earn its place toward that: a bilateral squat or hinge for
> maximal force, controlled tempo on the way down for the eccentric/braking
> quality that descents demand, and real trunk work for on-snow stability. The
> upper half keeps pulling/pressing strength for poles, packs and general
> robustness. In a cycling-heavy week, keep the leg load submaximal (see
> Scheduling) so it supports the riding rather than competing with it.

## Don't use when

- There is an acute injury in a region this session would load — swap to a
  split that avoids it (`gym/upper-body` if the legs are the problem,
  `gym/lower-isometric` if a joint needs low-damage loading) rather than
  training through it.
- Maximal leg force or a specific lower-body quality is the block's actual
  priority — then a dedicated `gym/lower-*` day beats a full-body compromise;
  full body spreads the same recovery budget across more muscle groups.
- The same day as power or intensive plyometric work — legs fatigued by a
  full-body session blunt the velocity those exist to train.

## Prescription

| Slot | Movement | Sets × reps | Intensity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Lower | Squat or hinge | 3–4 × 5–8 | RPE 7–8 | The leg driver; controlled 2–3 s lowering for braking quality |
| B. Upper push | Bench / overhead press | 3 × 6–10 | RPE 7–8 | — |
| C. Upper pull | Row / pull-up | 3 × 6–10 | RPE 7–8 | Balance the press volume |
| D. Trunk | Anti-rotation / carry / plank | 2–3 sets | quality | Ski-relevant trunk stiffness |
| E. Accessory | Single-leg or posterior chain | 2 × 8–12 | RPE 7 | Optional if time allows |

**Session shape:** one movement per pattern, biggest-to-smallest, 45–60 min.
Because the volume is spread across the whole body, no single region takes the
full dose — that is the trade for training everything in one slot.

## Dosing and progression

- **Double progression** per lift: hold the rep range, add reps across sets;
  once the top of the range is hit everywhere, add load and reset to the bottom.
- Rotate the lower-body driver across a block (squat → hinge → split squat) so
  the same tissue isn't hit identically every week.
- Progress **one lift at a time**, not the whole session at once — a bad week
  should tell you which movement to back off.
- **Regression path:** drop the leg driver's load/range → cut the accessory →
  fall back to `gym/upper-body` (protect the legs) or `gym/lower-isometric`
  (low-damage legs only).

## Scheduling

**Hard rules:**
- Not the same day as `gym/lower-power` or `plyometrics/intensive`.
- ≥48 h from power or intensive plyometric work in either direction.
- Not in race week for the leg component.

**Preferences:**
- Early-to-mid week, on a day with an easy aerobic session rather than a key one.
- If it shares a day with endurance, **lift first, ride/step second**, ≥6 h apart
  where the day allows.
- **In a cycling-heavy week, keep the leg half submaximal** (RPE 7, no grinding)
  so it supports the riding — the lower-body work eases off when cycling is the
  week's priority.
- Cycling the day after is fine; a hard run the next morning is not (legs will be
  moderately sore).

**What it costs the next 48 h:** moderate on every axis — no single axis is
maxed, but the spread means the legs and the pressed/pulled muscles all carry
some residue. Z2 riding the next day is comfortable; a key session is not.

## Interactions

- **With `gym/lower-max-strength` / `gym/lower-eccentric`:** overlapping leg
  stimulus. Rotate rather than stack — if a dedicated lower day is already the
  week's key strength session, full body is redundant that week.
- **With `cycling/endurance`:** the best pairing; low mechanical strain means the
  ride doesn't compete for the tissue this session loads.
- **With `gym/lower-power` / `plyometrics/intensive`:** protect their freshness —
  full-body leg fatigue is exactly what degrades explosive quality.
- **Interference:** this is a strength/hypertrophy stimulus, where concurrent
  endurance interference is not well supported (`principles/load-and-recovery`
  §2). Don't over-protect it from aerobic work.

## Stop rules

- Technique breakdown under load on any lift — that set ends, next set drops
  weight.
- Sharp joint, tendon or muscle-belly pain during a rep — end that exercise; do
  not substitute a similar movement in the same turn.
- A tendon reading above ~3/10, or worse the next morning — the dose was too high.
- Cannot hit last week's loads at the prescribed RPE — treat as a readiness
  signal, cut the session short rather than grinding.

## Worked example

> **Context:** this athlete, mid-August (strength-building phase toward the ski
> season), a busy week with a mountain day on Saturday, so only one gym slot fits
> and it has to cover legs and upper. Readiness normal.

> **Prescription — Tuesday:**
> - A. Back squat — 4 × 6 @ RPE 7, 3 s lowering
> - B. Overhead press — 3 × 8 @ RPE 7
> - C. Weighted pull-up / row — 3 × 8
> - D. Pallof press + suitcase carry — 3 sets each
> - E. Romanian deadlift — 2 × 10 @ RPE 7 (posterior chain, light)

> **Placement rationale:** one balanced session buys both legs and upper before a
> demanding Saturday on the hill, kept at RPE 7 so it doesn't leave the legs
> trashed for the mountain. Squat lowering is deliberately slow because descent
> braking is a ski-specific quality. Sits ≥48 h clear of any power/plyo day.

## Evidence notes

**Supported:**
- Concurrent endurance training does not meaningfully blunt whole-body strength
  or hypertrophy outcomes — the interference that matters is lower-body power
  (`principles/load-and-recovery` §2). A moderate full-body day schedules freely
  around endurance work.
- Heavy strength work improves cycling and running economy and economy
  durability (see `gym/lower-max-strength` Evidence notes); the leg half of this
  session carries that benefit at a lower per-session dose.

**Convention (defaults, thinly evidenced):**
- Treating full body as interchangeable with a split at matched weekly volume.
  Reasonable and widely used; the real driver is total quality sets per muscle
  group across the week, not the split.
- The specific RPE anchors and the 24 h spacing from a dedicated lower day —
  sensible coaching defaults, not tested thresholds.
