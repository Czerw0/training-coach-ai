# Gym — lower body, isometric

> DRAFT — coach to verify. General strength-and-conditioning guidance, not
> individualised medical advice.

## Use when

Building or maintaining lower-body strength with **held** contractions — wall
sit, Spanish squat, split-squat hold, isometric mid-thigh pull, single-leg calf
hold. High tension at almost no muscle-damage cost, which makes this the session
that keeps leg strength in when everything else about the week says "don't make
the legs sore":

- Near a hard endurance block, or inside a taper, when eccentric or heavy
  dynamic work would cost the next key session.
- Managing a grumpy patellar or Achilles tendon, where controlled loading is
  wanted but dynamic range is provoking symptoms.
- Holding strength through a congested in-season week with no recovery to spare.

## Don't use when

- An acute joint injury prevents finding a pain-free hold position. An isometric
  is only safe if the held angle is comfortable — if there is no such angle,
  this is not the session.
- Maximal dynamic force or hypertrophy is the actual goal and the athlete has
  the recovery budget for it. Isometrics build strength mostly **at the trained
  joint angle**; `gym/lower-max-strength` is the better tool when soreness isn't
  the constraint.

## Prescription

| Variant | Sets × reps | Load / duration | Rest | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yielding hold | 3–5 × 20–45 s | near-maximal, hold position | 90–120 s | Work capacity, tendon-friendly strength (Spanish squat, wall sit) |
| Overcoming (max) hold | 3–5 × 3–5 s | all-out push vs immovable resistance | 2 min | Maximal strength and rate of force (isometric mid-thigh pull) |
| Tendon-loading hold | 4–5 × 30–45 s | heavy, tolerable, pain ≤3/10 | 2 min | Progressive load on a reactive tendon (see Evidence notes) |

**Session shape:** one or two positions, 3–5 sets each. Vary the joint angle
across a block if broad strength is the goal, since gains concentrate near the
angle trained.

## Dosing and progression

- **Progress duration or load, then muscle length.** A longer hold or a heavier
  yielding position is a real step before moving to a more lengthened (harder)
  joint angle.
- Overcoming holds progress by **intent**, not time — 3–5 s of genuine maximal
  effort is the whole set; adding seconds just adds fatigue.
- Because damage is low, this session can be run more frequently than any other
  strength option without a soreness penalty.
- **Regression path:** shorten the hold → reduce load → reduce range / find an
  easier joint angle → rest the joint.

## Scheduling

**Hard rules** (breaking these makes the plan wrong):
- Only prescribe if a pain-free hold angle exists (the contraindication above).

**Preferences** (trade these away when the calendar forces it):
- This is the low-cost leg session — use it *because* it has no spacing or
  same-day conflicts. It is the answer to "keep leg strength without touching
  the next hard day."
- Excellent the day before or after hard running or cycling, and a mainstay
  during a taper when heavier leg work is pulled.
- If a tendon is being managed, this can run most days at a tolerable load —
  progression is by load and symptom response, not by weekly spacing.

**What it costs:** moderate neural tension, negligible damage or mechanical
strain. Clears inside a day; it will not compromise tomorrow's key session.

## Interactions

- **With every endurance session:** the standout pairing. No same-day conflict
  and no spacing requirement — that is the point of the session.
- **With `gym/lower-max-strength`:** overlapping goal, different cost. Use this
  as the low-soreness maintenance mode, or as the regression target when heavy
  dynamic work needs backing off.
- **Interference:** a strength stimulus, so the interference worry is minimal
  (see `principles/load-and-recovery` §2). The reason to reach for it is low
  soreness, not interference protection.

## Stop rules

- Pain rising above ~3/10 during a hold, or worse the next morning — the dose
  or the angle was wrong; reduce load or find a more comfortable position.
- Loss of position (the athlete sags out of the hold) — the set is over; that is
  fatigue, not something to grind through.
- Cramp in the held muscle — end the set, the tension is no longer productive.
- Any sharp or localised joint pain — stop, and reassess whether a pain-free
  angle actually exists.

## Worked example

> **Context:** runner, taper week before a half-marathon, wants to keep some
> leg strength without adding soreness; mild patellar-tendon niggle after
> downhill sessions, currently 2/10 and not limiting.

> **Prescription — Wednesday, easy day:**
> - Spanish squat — 4 × 30 s, near-maximal, 90 s rest
> - Split-squat isometric hold — 3 × 25 s/side, mid-range, pain-free
> - Single-leg calf hold — 3 × 30 s/side
> - Keep every position pain ≤3/10; stop any that provokes the tendon.

> **Placement rationale:** taper rules out eccentric and heavy dynamic leg work
> (`principles/planning-the-week` §6), but strength maintenance is still wanted.
> Isometrics deliver tension with no DOMS, so this sits on an easy day two days
> out from the race without touching race-day legs, and the tolerable tendon
> load is more useful here than rest.

## Evidence notes

**Supported:**
- Isometric training increases strength most at or near the **trained joint
  angle**, with smaller carry-over to other angles — vary positions for broad
  strength.
- For painful tendons, **progressive tendon loading combined with isometric
  work, and heavy slow resistance, outperform eccentric training alone** for
  long-term outcomes. Isometrics belong in the better-supported tendon approach.

**Contested — state carefully to the athlete:**
- The popular claim that a single bout of heavy isometrics reliably produces
  **immediate** analgesia in reactive tendinopathy is **not consistently
  replicated** — some trials show acute pain reduction, others find no advantage
  over other loading. Prescribe isometrics as tolerable progressive loading, not
  as a guaranteed pain switch.

**Convention (defaults, thinly evidenced):**
- The specific 20–45 s yielding and 3–5 s overcoming durations, and the 4–5 ×
  ~45 s tendon protocol. Sensible, widely used, not tested thresholds.
