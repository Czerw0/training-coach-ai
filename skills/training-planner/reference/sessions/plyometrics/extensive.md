# Plyometrics — extensive

> DRAFT — coach to verify. General strength-and-conditioning guidance, not
> individualised medical advice.

## Use when

Building elastic quality, foot and ankle stiffness, and tissue tolerance with
**many low-intensity ground contacts** — pogo hops, ankling, low skips,
line/rope hops, low box step-offs. This is the foundation that earns the right
to prescribe `plyometrics/intensive`, a cheap way to keep reactive quality
year-round, and effective movement prep inside a warm-up.

- Preparing a runner or jumper for higher-amplitude plyometrics over the coming
  weeks.
- Maintaining stiffness in-season without the cost of intensive work.
- Adding a small reactive stimulus to a warm-up before running or lifting.

## Don't use when

- There is active bone stress or reactive Achilles symptoms. Low intensity still
  means repeated impact, and volume is the problem for bone and tendon.
- The goal is maximal reactive output on fresh legs — that is
  `plyometrics/intensive`, not this. Extensive work builds the base, it doesn't
  peak the quality.

## Prescription

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pogo hops | 3–4 × 15–20 | 45–60 s | Stiff ankle, short ground contact, quiet |
| Ankling / low skips | 3–4 × 20–30 m | 60 s | Rhythm and foot contact, not height |
| Line / rope hops | 3–4 × 20–30 | 45 s | Bilateral → single-leg as tolerated |
| Low box step-off | 3 × 8–10 | 60 s | Low height, soft controlled landing |

**Volume: roughly 100–200 low-intensity ground contacts total**, in short crisp
sets with brief rest, quality held throughout. Effort lives in the stiffness and
rhythm, not in maximal height.

## Dosing and progression

- **Add contacts, then complexity, before intensity.** More contacts → bilateral
  to single-leg → directional — height and amplitude come last (that transition
  *is* the move to `plyometrics/intensive`).
- Cue short ground-contact time, tall posture, symmetrical and quiet landings,
  absorb through the whole foot.
- Progress weekly contacts gradually — this is impact loading even at low
  intensity, and bone and Achilles tolerance adapt slowly.
- **Regression path:** fewer contacts → bilateral only → lower amplitude → rest
  the impact tissue.

## Scheduling

**Hard rules:**
- None that constrain placement — no same-day conflicts and no spacing
  requirements. Its value is precisely that it fits almost anywhere.

**Preferences:**
- Frequent: 2–3×/week is fine, including as warm-up movement prep before running
  or lifting.
- Use it on the ramp toward intensive plyos or a running block, and in-season to
  keep stiffness cheaply.
- Even though it fits freely, keep an eye on **total weekly impact** when it is
  stacked alongside running — the contacts add to the same bone/tendon budget.

**What it costs:** low on every axis, clears within ~12 h. The only real cost is
cumulative impact when weekly contacts climb too fast.

## Interactions

- **With `plyometrics/intensive`:** this is the on-ramp and the maintenance
  mode; intensive is a block built on top of a clean tolerance of this.
- **With `running/*`:** fine as prep in the same warm-up as an easy or interval
  run, but its contacts count toward the week's total impact — don't treat it as
  free when running volume is already high.
- **With `cycling/endurance` and `gym/upper-body`:** no competition for tissue;
  pairs freely.
- **Interference:** negligible. Too low-intensity to threaten any endurance
  outcome or to need protecting from one.

## Stop rules

- Any bony tenderness or Achilles/patellar pain during or after — stop, and do
  not add impact volume until it settles.
- Landings turning heavy or loud, or ground-contact time lengthening → stiffness
  has failed under fatigue; end the session, that quality is the point.
- Pain that worsens across the session rather than warming up → stop, and route
  a bone-stress suspicion to a clinician.

## Worked example

> **Context:** recreational runner, base phase, no plyometric history, wants to
> add reactive work to eventually support interval running. No current
> symptoms. Running 4×/week.

> **Prescription — twice this week, in the warm-up before easy runs:**
> - Pogo hops — 3 × 15 (45 contacts)
> - Ankling — 3 × 20 m
> - Line hops (bilateral) — 3 × 20 (60 contacts)
> - ~120 total contacts; all low, quiet, short-contact. Stop on any niggle.

> **Placement rationale:** attaching it to existing easy-run warm-ups adds the
> reactive stimulus at near-zero scheduling cost and no extra sessions. Volume
> starts near the bottom of the 100–200 band because there is no plyometric base
> yet — contacts, not amplitude, are what progresses over the next few weeks
> before `plyometrics/intensive` is even considered.

## Evidence notes

**Supported:**
- Dose-response evidence supports **starting low-volume and low-amplitude
  (≈40–60 contacts) and progressing to higher-amplitude work over weeks** — this
  session is that starting point.
- Low-intensity plyometrics need **at least ~24 h** between exposures, far less
  than the 48–72 h high-amplitude work demands, which is why extensive work can
  run frequently.
- Progressive plyometric programmes improve jump and sprint performance; the
  extensive base is where that progression begins safely.

**Convention (defaults, thinly evidenced):**
- The specific 100–200 contact band. A widely used coaching range for extensive
  work, not a validated threshold — landing quality and symptom response are the
  better guides.
