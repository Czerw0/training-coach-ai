# Plyometrics — intensive

> DRAFT — coach to verify. General strength-and-conditioning guidance, not
> individualised medical advice.

> **This athlete —** a **build-toward target, not a right-now session.** The
> athlete is only just returning to plyometrics, so the default is
> `plyometrics/extensive` until several weeks of clean tolerance are logged
> (prerequisite 2 below). When intensive work does come in, keep the priority on
> **jump height and landing quality** and on **speed** (short ground contact,
> reactive stiffness) — those are the ski-relevant qualities — and progress
> **volume slowly** (contacts before amplitude). Do not rush box height or
> single-leg work; the return is capped by tissue tolerance, not by motivation.

## Use when

Maximal reactive output from few, high-quality ground contacts: depth jumps,
bounding, single-leg max hops, hurdle jumps. Powerful for reactive strength and
running economy — and the session in this library most likely to injure someone
if placed on tired legs.

Prescribe only when **all three** prerequisites hold:
1. A genuine strength base (commonly cited: squat ~1.5 × bodyweight, or clean
   controlled single-leg landing under fatigue).
2. Several weeks of `plyometrics/extensive` tolerated cleanly.
3. Good readiness today, no lingering DOMS.

If any one is missing, prescribe `plyometrics/extensive` instead and say why.

## Don't use when

- The athlete is in a high running-volume block. Combined impact on bone,
  Achilles and patellar tendon accumulates faster than either alone.
- In a taper or race week.
- After heavy lower-body work or a hard run, on the same day or the day after.
- Any bone-stress or reactive-tendon symptoms are present.

## Prescription

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Rest | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth jump | 3–5 × 3–5 | 2–3 min | Start at 20–30 cm box; height progresses last |
| Bounding | 3–4 × 20–30 m | 2–3 min | Distance per contact, not speed |
| Single-leg max hop | 3 × 3–5/side | 2–3 min | Highest demand — introduce last |
| Hurdle jump | 3–4 × 4–6 | 2 min | Height set so landing stays controlled |

**Volume: 20–40 high-intensity ground contacts per session, total.** Novices
start nearer 40–60 low-amplitude contacts and earn their way down to true
intensive work. Full recovery between sets (1–3 min) — this is not a
conditioning session.

**Quality gate:** stop the exercise the moment jump height, bound distance or
ground-contact time visibly degrades. A fatigued intensive plyo delivers no
adaptation and all of the injury exposure.

## Dosing and progression

Progress in this order, one step at a time:

```
contacts  →  complexity (bilateral → unilateral → directional)  →  intensity (box height, bound distance)
```

- Add roughly 10–20 contacts per week, not per session.
- Box height is the **last** variable to move and the one that most often gets
  moved first. Resist it.
- **Regression path:** reduce contacts → reduce box height → move to
  `plyometrics/extensive`.

## Scheduling

**Hard rules:**
- Fresh legs: nothing heavy or hard on the legs in the preceding 48 h.
- 72 h between intensive plyometric sessions.
- ≥48 h from hard running intervals, power work and heavy lower-body gym.
- Not in a taper or race week.
- First in the session, immediately after a full warm-up — never after
  conditioning.

**Preferences:**
- Early in the week and early in the session.
- 1–2 sessions per week maximum; 2–3 weekly is a plyometric *programme* ceiling
  and should mostly be extensive work.
- Pair the next day with upper body or Z2 cycling.

**What it costs the next 48 h:** high `neural` and high `mechanical`. It feels
cheap because the volume is tiny and the athlete isn't out of breath — that
mismatch is exactly why it gets misplaced.

## Interactions

- **With `plyometrics/extensive`:** extensive is the on-ramp and the maintenance
  mode. Intensive is a block, not a year-round staple.
- **With `gym/lower-power`:** overlapping neural demand and the same freshness
  requirement. Either separate by 48 h or combine them into a single "fresh
  legs" day with power first, plyos second, and total volume cut.
- **With `running/easy-long`:** low-volume easy running the day after is
  acceptable; a long run or intervals is not.
- **Interference:** power and explosive strength are the outcomes where the
  interference effect is genuinely supported. This is the session to protect
  from surrounding endurance work — unlike strength or hypertrophy sessions,
  which schedule far more freely.

## Stop rules

- Jump height or bound distance dropping → exercise over.
- Audible or heavy landings, knees collapsing inward → technique has failed
  under fatigue; stop and regress next session.
- Any tendon or bony pain, at any intensity → stop, and do not substitute
  another impact session in its place.
- Patellar or Achilles pain the morning after → the dose was too high; halve
  contacts and reassess before the next exposure.

## Worked example

> **Context:** 800 m runner, pre-competition phase, 3 months of consistent gym
> and extensive plyos, squats 1.6 × bodyweight, no current symptoms. Week has
> intervals Wednesday and a race Saturday in 3 weeks' time.

> **Prescription — Monday, after full warm-up, before anything else:**
> - Pogo hops 2 × 10 (potentiation, from `plyometrics/extensive`)
> - Depth jump from 30 cm — 4 × 4, 2.5 min rest (16 contacts)
> - Bounding — 3 × 25 m, 3 min rest (~18 contacts)
> - Total high-intensity contacts: ~34
> - Stop rule stated to athlete: any rep noticeably lower or slower ends the set.

> **Placement rationale:** Monday gives 48 h to Wednesday's intervals and comes
> after Sunday's rest day, so the legs are as fresh as the week allows. Contacts
> sit at the top of the 20–40 band because the base is well established. This
> session gets pulled entirely once the taper starts.

## Evidence notes

**Supported:**
- High-intensity plyometric sessions warrant **48–72 h** between exposures; low-
  intensity work needs at least 24 h. High-amplitude plyometrics produce
  eccentric muscle damage comparable to heavy resistance training, which is why
  24 h spacing is too short.
- 1–3 sessions per week is the standard programming range across sources.
- Progressive plyometric programmes performed twice weekly improve jump and
  sprint performance over 7–10 weeks.
- Dose-response evidence supports starting low-volume and low-amplitude
  (40–60 contacts) and progressing to higher-amplitude work over weeks.
- Adding plyometrics alongside strength work improved running economy durability
  and fatigued high-intensity performance in well-trained runners.

**Convention:**
- The specific 20–40 contact band for intensive work, and the 1.5 × bodyweight
  squat prerequisite. Both are widely cited coaching standards rather than
  validated thresholds — useful defaults, not gates to defend rigidly. Landing
  quality is the better real test.
