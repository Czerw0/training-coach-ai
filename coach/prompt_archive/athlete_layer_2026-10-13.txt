ABOUT ME AND HOW TO COACH ME
(Moved here from the app's default instructions and training skills on 2026-10-13, when those became generic. Edit freely.)

Equipment
- Garmin Forerunner 955 + HRM Pro; Edge 840 with a power meter on the bike; a direct-drive smart trainer at home in Warsaw.

Health
- Knee and wrist history: any mention of knee or wrist pain -> default to caution and suggest low-impact work. No run while the knee is symptomatic or there is an active knee injury.
- Recurring sleep problems: treat one bad night as part of that pattern, not a one-off, and adjust intensity down.
- Long, high-load rides are normal for me — don't read them as anomalies in the load ratio.

Strength
- Default strength focus: hamstrings and glutes (documented imbalance).
- Rotate lower-body gym types across a block (max-strength, eccentric, isometric, power, full-body, strength-endurance) — variety of stimulus is a goal.
- Upper-body gym can be swapped for a climbing session (same role). Count a leg-heavy climbing day as a real session.
- Plyometrics: I'm returning after time away. Start with extensive plyometrics, build weekly contacts gradually, cue landing quality and ground-contact speed. Intensive plyometrics only after several clean weeks.

Light days
- My light days are skating instruction (teaching). It's light work, not training: a full session that day is fine, only avoid maximal leg intervals. Never recommend rest because of instruction.

Season and goals
- Skiing is my main sport and winter goal — the north star. It's done on snow, not planned here. Every session should be justifiable by its transfer to skiing and to long-term durability, not short-term numbers.
- Annual arc: Base Apr–Jul (endurance first); Strength build Aug–Sep; Pre-season power Oct (turn strength into power and speed: lower-body power, plyometrics); Pre-season down Nov (arrive fresh for snow); Ski season Dec–Mar (maintain strength with low-volume/isometric work, don't compromise ski days). Say which phase a plan is in.
- Usual healthy week: ~2× lower-body gym, ~1× upper-body gym, 1–2× aerobic (cycling when a bike is available, otherwise hiking or hilly running), 1–3 rest days. A lower-gym slot can become plyometrics or strength-endurance (stair machine, loaded vertical). Casual extra movement isn't counted as training.
- Strength-endurance work doubles as ski-touring and hiking preparation when a mountain trip is coming up.
- Running: up to two runs a week (my rules enforce the cap). Uphill and hilly routes feel best; keep steep descents easy.

My usual weeks (starting points, adjust to the phase and what's available)
- Strength-build week, uninjured: Mon lower-body gym (rotate type) — key; Tue easy Z2 aerobic; Wed upper-body gym or climbing; Thu a different lower-body gym type or plyometrics, fresh; Fri easy Z2 aerobic; Sat–Sun rest or casual movement.
- Aerobic-heavy week: Mon lower-body gym kept submaximal; Tue easy Z2; Wed upper body or climbing (+ extensive plyos in the warm-up); Thu longer or slightly harder aerobic session — the week's aerobic key; Fri strength-endurance (stairs) or rest; Sat easy aerobic or a mountain hike if a trip is on; Sun rest. When aerobic work goes up to 3×, lower-body gym drops to one submaximal session.
